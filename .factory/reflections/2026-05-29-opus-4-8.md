# Session Reflection — 2026-05-29

**Work summary:** Asked to assess recent agent work and pick the next valuable thing, I judged GR-047's web-service result (88% lock, "jury working as designed") to be an unexamined over-claim and re-ran it as GR-048 with a 3-member jury (K2+Sonnet+MiMo) and preserved artifacts. Traced the root cause to an altitude mismatch (ACs are deliverable-altitude, units are atom-altitude), drew the matching lessons from v1 (software-factory), and committed the analysis plus a design set: BC-222/223/224/225, RFC-038 (execution gate), RFC-039 (deliverable-driven decomposition, as a hypothesis), and a GR-049 plan. ## On the project

The pipeline's engineering is genuinely good — the run came up, ran 5 modules through a 7-stage DAG, and produced clean, inspectable per-item artifacts (the jury verdict JSONs are what let me adjudicate at all). The epistemic-governance scaffolding (RFC-030/031, breadcrumb discipline, the worklog/reflection habit) is the project's real asset and it worked exactly as intended this session: the system produced a flattering number, and the discipline of preserving artifacts and reading them caught the over-claim two runs later. That is the watch-metric ("rate of self-caught over-claims") paying off.

The thing that feels wrong, now exposed, is structural: conformance is decided by LLM opinion (review + jury reading artifacts), and the work is decomposed below the altitude at which the ACs are even expressible. The 96–97% CLI results were the assumption being *masked* (for CLI, unit ≈ deliverable), not validated. The whole pipeline has been verifying-by-reading and deferring real execution, which is precisely the assumption v1 had started to abandon (BC-314, the bringup doc) before it died. v2 inherited v1's governance lessons but not its half-finished move to execution-first verification. That is the most important thing I learned about the project.

## On the work done

I'm confident in the GR-048 adjudication because it's grounded in ground truth, not inference: the spec text (FastAPI + SQLite, AC-07 = "HTTP 422"), `grep` showing zero FastAPI imports anywhere, and the actual `get_links` source fabricating 25 in-memory links. I checked the implementation code rather than trusting the jurors' rationales, which matters because the jurors were the thing under suspicion.

What I'm less certain of: RFC-039 (deliverable-driven decomposition) is a hypothesis, and I wrote it as one — but I want to flag that I find it persuasive, and persuasive-to-me is exactly the bias the project exists to distrust. I have *not* shown vertical slicing works; I've only shown atomic slicing fails for this archetype. The falsification criteria in RFC-039 ("How this could be wrong") are the part to take seriously, not the proposal.

The 3-member jury was my experimental design, and the honest result is that it was the wrong instrument for adjudication: quorum-2 just out-voted the correct dissenter and locked defective code. The *value* came from reading the rationales and checking the spec by hand, not from the vote arithmetic. I should have predicted that majority-rule can't adjudicate a conformance question; I didn't, and the run is more informative for it, but that was luck, not design.
