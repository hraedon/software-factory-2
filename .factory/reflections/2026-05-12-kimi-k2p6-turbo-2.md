## On the project

The throughput improvement story is real. GR-015's 0% first-attempt rate was primarily ruff-shaped, and the auto-fix-back mechanism eliminates that entirely. The remaining failures are import errors (model generates code referencing dependencies incorrectly) and occasional mypy/pytest issues. These are harder to auto-fix and may need Phase 4 mechanisms (self-critique, race).

The pipeline is becoming more self-healing. The inner gate now acts as a silent janitor — the model produces messy code, ruff cleans it up, and the gate passes. This is good for throughput but means the model never learns to generate clean code. The pre-flight checklists attempt to teach the model, but their effect is hard to isolate from the auto-fix.

One architectural concern: the inner gate is accumulating subprocess calls. `_run_ruff_fast` now makes 4 ruff invocations per artifact (F841 fix, safe fix, format, verify). For a clean artifact, 3 of those are no-ops. At ~0.5s each, that's ~2s overhead per work item. Not significant now, but worth watching.

**Confident in:** The three review fixes. Catching the ruleset divergence before GR-019 was important — it would have been a silent footgun. The targeted F841 fix is more maintainable than blanket `--unsafe-fixes`. The `.orig` backup is a small but principled addition.

**Less confident in:** The pre-flight checklists. I can't tell if the 64% first-attempt rate is due to checklists, auto-fix, or model variance. An A/B test (checklists vs no-checklists, same auto-fix) would be needed to isolate the effect, and that's probably not worth the model budget.

**Not sure about:** Whether the 1 stuck item (cert_chain_library implementation timing out at 600s) is a model capability issue or a prompt issue. The cert-chain PKCS7 parsing is genuinely complex. A shorter timeout with more retries might work better than a single long timeout.
