## On the project

sf2's epistemic-governance thesis held up under real pressure this session, and that's the thing worth saying plainly. GR-055's defining failure — mechanical gates rubber-stamping a vacuous async test (100% inner_pytest, 6% cross-family review) — was a *textbook* instance of the over-claim the whole project exists to catch. The fix (execute the boot probe upstream; make worker tests un-vacuous-able) is the right shape, and GR-058 showed the conformance/boot path that ran **zero times** in 055 now executing and gating. The architecture is doing what it claims.

What still feels fragile: the conformance gate's spec parsing leans on a heading-style *fallback* after a YAML parse that fails loudly-at-debug on every substrate spec (`gate/conformance.py` `_extract_acs_from_spec`). It works, but it's one spec-format drift away from silently extracting zero ACs and short-circuiting the gate. And the run harness is brittle around the edges — three different infra failures (pip-less gate venv, missing agent-wake config, cleanup exit-1) surfaced across three runs, none of them pipeline logic, all of them costing runs or muddying signal.

agent-notes is in good shape but its real work is strategic, not feature work — the Plan 007 thread (DB-canonical, hook-enforced lifecycle, opencode tier-one) is the thing that makes "memory layer" true rather than aspirational. I built the testable substrate; the load-bearing half (enforcement hooks) needs a live harness to validate.
